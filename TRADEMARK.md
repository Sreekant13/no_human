# Trademark policy

The MIT Licence in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) gives you broad rights over the *code*.
It says nothing about the *name*. This page says what you may and may not do
with "no_human" and with the project's logo and visual identity.

The short version: use the name to describe accurately what your thing is or
what it works with. Do not use it in a way that suggests your thing is this
project, or is endorsed by it.

## The mark

The name "no_human", and the project's logo and wordmark — including the mark
drawn by [`web/src/Logo.jsx`](web/src/Logo.jsx) and whatever succeeds it — are
unregistered marks of eyalgolan, the maintainer of this project. No
registration is claimed and none has been applied for. This is a statement of
the maintainer's policy over an unregistered mark, not an assertion of a
registered right.

The maintainer also intends to offer a paid hosted service under the same name.
The name covers both the open source project and that service; the two are not
separate marks.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your software works with.** "Compatible with no_human", "an
  integration for no_human", "a plugin for no_human", "built on no_human". This
  is nominative use and it is exactly what a name is for.
- **Say that you forked it.** "A fork of no_human" is accurate and fine. So is
  "based on no_human".
- **Write about it.** Articles, tutorials, videos, conference talks,
  comparisons, reviews — favourable or not. Use the logo to illustrate them.
- **Redistribute unmodified builds** under the name, so long as they really are
  unmodified.
- **Use the name in your own repository's prose**, in a `README`, in
  documentation, and in dependency listings.

Where you use the name this way, keep it as a plain word — same font and weight
as the surrounding text — rather than styling it to look like the project's own
branding.

## What needs permission

- **Naming a modified version after it.** Ship your fork under its own name. Do
  not call a modified build "no_human", and do not call it something that reads
  as an official variant — "no_human Pro", "no_human Enterprise", "no_human
  Cloud", "no_human for X".
- **Names built from the mark.** Domains, product names, package names,
  company names, app-store listings, and social accounts of the form
  `nohuman-<something>` or `<something>-no_human` where a reader would take it
  as official.
- **The logo as your own.** Do not use the logo or wordmark as the icon or
  identity of your product, your company, or your service. Do not modify the
  logo and present the result as the project's.
- **Suggesting endorsement.** Do not describe your product, service, or
  organisation as official, certified, affiliated, partnered, or endorsed. It
  is not, unless there is something in writing saying it is.
- **A hosted service under the name.** Running the software as a service is
  permitted by the licence; doing it *under this name* is not covered by this
  policy.

## Forks

The licence lets you fork this project and always will. This page does not
limit that in any way — it limits only what you call the result.

A fork that keeps the name is confusing to users who cannot tell whose bug they
are filing, and it is confusing to the maintainer for the same reason. Pick your
own name, and say plainly in your README that it began as a fork of no_human.
That is better for you as well: your fork gets to be its own thing.

## Other people's marks

This project names a lot of software it works with, and those names belong to
other people.

**Who owns what is recorded in one place — [`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`](THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md),
under "Third-party trademarks" — and deliberately not repeated here.** A list kept
in two files goes wrong in one of them, and an attribution that names the wrong
owner is itself a misuse of the mark. One copy, one place to fix.

Every one of those names is used **nominatively** — to say truthfully what this
software runs on or talks to. That is what the names are for, and it is the same
courtesy this project asks for its own name, above.

None of it implies affiliation, sponsorship, partnership, certification, or
endorsement by any of those companies. There is none. Where this project runs on
a third party's model or connects to a third party's service, you bring your own
account and your own credentials, and your relationship with that company is
yours and governed by their terms, not by anything here.

Two rules follow, and they bind this project as much as anyone forking it:

- **Their logos are not ours to ship.** This project displays no vendor's logo,
  official or redrawn. Redrawing a mark is not the cautious option — it adds a
  modification breach, and often a copyright claim, on top of the trademark
  question. Several of these owners forbid third-party logo use outright or require
  written permission. Names in text are what nominative use covers; artwork is not.
- **No claim of endorsement.** This project is not a partner, an official
  integration, or certified by anyone.

If you own one of these marks and something here misuses it, open an issue and it
will be fixed.

## Asking

Anything not covered above, or anything you are unsure about: open an issue and
ask. Permission is usually not a problem when the use is honest about what it
is.

This policy may change. Changes are not retroactive against a use that was
permitted when you started it, but they do apply to what you do afterwards.
